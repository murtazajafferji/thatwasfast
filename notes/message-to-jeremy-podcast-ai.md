# Message to Jeremy — AI for podcast editing / audio / video

Great question, and it changes my answer a bit — so let me separate two things, because they're different tools and people mix them up constantly.

**Claude Cowork does not edit audio or video.** It's not that it's bad at it; it can't do it. It works with files, documents, and text. So for the podcast itself you want a real media tool. Cowork is still the right pick for the business side — it just isn't the editor.

Good news: the editing side is where AI has gotten genuinely, stupidly good. This is the one area where a non-technical person can now do what used to take a hired editor.

---

## The one tool to start with: Descript

This is the big one for podcasting. The core trick: **it transcribes your episode, and then you edit the audio and video by editing the text.** Delete a sentence in the transcript, it's gone from the recording. No waveforms, no timeline, no learning curve.

What it does automatically:
- **Removes filler words** — every "um," "uh," "you know" across the whole episode in one click
- **Studio Sound** — makes a mediocre mic in a normal room sound like a treated studio. This one genuinely sounds like magic the first time.
- **Removes retakes** — you flub a line, say it again, it detects the flub and cuts it
- **Shortens dead air** between words
- **Eye Contact** — corrects your gaze on video so you look at the camera while reading notes
- **Auto-generates clips** — finds the good moments and cuts them into vertical clips for Instagram/TikTok/YouTube Shorts, captioned
- **Writes your show notes, chapters, titles, and social posts** from the episode
- **Multicam** — if you record two angles or two people, it cuts between speakers automatically

**Cost (annual):** Free tier to try (1 hr/month). **Hobbyist $16/mo** (10 hrs/month). **Creator $24/mo** (30 hrs/month) — Creator is the realistic one for a regular show, mostly for the AI credits and extra hours.

## Free things worth knowing about

**Adobe Podcast — Enhance Speech.** Completely free, no account needed. Upload a noisy, echoey recording and it comes back sounding like a studio mic. If you only ever use one AI audio tool, this is the highest quality-per-effort of anything out there. Worth running on old episodes just to hear the difference.

**Descript's free tier.** 1 hour of media a month. Enough to edit one episode and decide if you like it before paying.

---

## What I'd actually do

1. **Take an old episode you already have** and run it through Adobe Podcast Enhance (free). Takes two minutes. It'll tell you instantly whether the audio problem you remember is still a problem.
2. **Sign up for Descript free** and edit one episode by editing text. If the workflow clicks for you, it'll cut your editing time by something like 70%.
3. **Then pick a plan** — $16 if you're doing a couple episodes a month, $24 if you're going weekly and want the clips.
4. **Use Cowork for the surrounding work** — guest research and prep questions, episode outline, follow-up emails, repurposing the transcript into a newsletter or LinkedIn post.

Total: **$20 Claude + ~$16-24 Descript.** Call it $40/month to run the business side and the show, which is roughly one hour of a freelance editor's time.

---

## The honest caveats

- **Studio Sound and filler-word removal can over-process.** If you cut every single "um" the result can sound unnaturally clipped and a little robotic. Leave some in. Your ear is the judge, not the tool.
- **AI-generated clips are a starting point, not a finished product.** It's decent at finding interesting moments and bad at knowing where a thought actually ends. Expect to adjust the in/out points.
- **The AI voice cloning stuff** (type a word you never said and it speaks in your voice) works, but use it lightly — it's for fixing a mispronounced name, not rewriting what you said.
- **Descript bills by media hours**, so a long unedited recording eats your allowance faster than you'd expect. Don't upload four hours of raw tape for a 40-minute show.
